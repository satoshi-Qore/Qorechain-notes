# Post-Quantum Cryptography Notes

Post-quantum cryptography focuses on cryptographic systems designed to remain secure against future quantum-capable attackers.

## Areas of Interest

- ML-DSA for quantum-resistant digital signatures
- ML-KEM for quantum-resistant key encapsulation
- SHAKE-256 and extendable-output hash functions
- Quantum-resistant identity and transaction signing
- Migration paths from classical cryptography to post-quantum systems

## Why It Matters

Most current blockchains rely on classical cryptography. If large-scale quantum computers become practical, some existing signature schemes could become vulnerable. Post-quantum systems aim to prepare infrastructure before that risk becomes urgent.

## Blockchain Relevance

Post-quantum cryptography can affect several parts of blockchain infrastructure:

- Wallet signatures
- Validator identity
- Node authentication
- Long-term transaction security
- Cross-chain messaging and bridge security

## Practical Research Notes

When studying post-quantum blockchain infrastructure, useful questions include:

- Which algorithms are standardized and production-ready?
- How large are signatures and keys compared with classical systems?
- What are the performance tradeoffs for nodes and validators?
- How can community users understand PQC without heavy mathematics?
